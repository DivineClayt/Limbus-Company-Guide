Limbus Company is a real-time turn-based RPG where you command a team of 12 people, referred in-game to as "Sinners". This guide is designed to provide you basic mechanical knowledge, alongside some unique interactions depending on certain things you do.

## The Basics

The basics of the game is simple. The game is split into cantos, as of current, there are 10 canto's in the game, meaning there's 10 chapters to play through. There are usually three types of nodes you will see the most. Story nodes, represented by a book, Combat Nodes, represented by a sword, and story+combat nodes, represented by a mixture of the symbols.

## Enkephalin

Enkephalin is your primary resource in the game. You mainly use it to enter combat nodes, Once you run out, you need to let it recharge before you can go into more combat nodes.

Enkephalin is also not just simply for entering nodes. When you click on the enkephalin bar you get a menu brought up, you can then convert your enkephalin into **modules**. These modules allow you to enter dungeons, luxcavations or mirror dungeons.

There also exists something called enkephalin boxes, which are only obtained via the battle pass or bokgak events, you can break them down for 60 enkephalin per box

Your enkephalin cap increases the more you level up, so early on you should focus on getting your manager level up to increase your cap for more enkephalin each day
## Lunacy

Lunacy is the primary currency for Limbus Company. You use it to pull from the banners in the extraction page. You can also use it (if its paid lunacy) to obtain the season's battle pass, you may also use lunacy to revive your entire team if they all die without resetting your progress in the encounter

## Identities

In Gacha Games, you pull characters to obtain them with each having new and different abilities. Limbus Company follows the same idea, but instead of being different characters, it is different Identities (or IDs for short). Each ID has a different set of skills and different set of mechanics for how they work. ID's are effectively "what if's" of your sinner's, like what if X sinner replaced this person? Each Identity typically has atleast 1 status effect related to them, though some have up to 3 status effects, and some of the more earlier ones have no primary status effects associated.

Each identity has four skills. Skill 1 (S1), Skill 2 (S2), Skill 3 (S3) and Defense

Some Identities may have multiple forms of S1, S2 or S3, and its entirely dependent, most of the time ID's will use different forms of their S1, 2 or 3 if they meet the right conditional, which changes the skill entirely.

On the dashboard, your skills are cycled, with you having access to three S1's, two S2's, and one S3.

These then get cycled around randomly, so you might get some skills earlier or later, or you might get two copies of the same skill.

In limbus, the game is based around your skills, and each skill has a number of coins.

Coins is your chances for clashing, and how many opportunities you get to raise the power of your skill.

To sum up clashing, bigger number is better. Lets use the downward slash from above as an example.

It has a base power of three, and has a 7 in coin power. So if you roll heads, you add the coin power to the base power, and that becomes your final power. This is then compared against the enemies skill, if its lower, you lose 1 coin, if you have more, this continues until one person runs out of coins on their skill.

The person who loses the clash has their skill cancelled, and the person who won goes ahead with their skill. This is the bread and butter of limbus combat. But there is also defense skills, an alternative you can use by clicking on your character portrait on the combat dashboard, which replaces your bottom skill.

Defense skills come in a variety of forms:

Guard: You gain shield HP equal to the power
Clashable Guard: You clash with the attack, if you fail you still get to mitigate damage
Counter: You take the attack from the opponent and attack back after unopposed
Clashable Counter: You clash with the attack, if you win you get to attack back at them, no effect if you fail
Evade: You dodge the attack, you roll your evade coin against their attack, once per coin on their attack, if your evade value is higher, you dodge, if its lower, you get hit and cant evade for the rest of the attack



## E.G.O
E.G.O is like the ultimate move of a sinner, it is essentially a high rolling skill that costs sin resources to be able to use. As of current, a sinner can have up to four E.G.O skills equipped at once. E.G.O comes in five levels, although ALEPH ego's have not been introduced to the game yet.:
ZAYIN
TETH
HE
WAW
ALEPH

Typically, the higher level an E.G.O skill is, the more complicated/stronger it is. 

You can only have one E.G.O of each level equipped, so you cannot have 2 ZAYIN ego's equipped, but you may have 1 ZAYIN and 1 TETH ego equipped at the same time.

When you use an E.G.O, the sinner activates the passive effect of the E.G.O from that turn onwards, which may vary depending on what it is. As of recent some more newer E.G.O's have a condition that if a specific ID is using the E.G.O, it gets boosted/gets special effects for it.

## Levels and Threadspinning

Each Identity has its own respective level, the current cap is level 65, but your cap is lower for earlier on, but each canto after canto 5 provides +5 to your level cap.

The higher a persons level is, the better they will be able to clash with higher level enemies, so just make sure you improve the level of your identities.

Improving your ID's levels improved their offense and defense levels, alongside their health.

Each ID, besides its level has an associated **uptie level**, when you uptie an identity, they go up an uptie level. Uptie Levels are different from regular Levels, as while Regular levels increase your ID's overall strength, uptie level increases the quality of your ID can do, and it gives improvements for your skills across the board. 

Uptie Level 1 is the lowest level you can have on an ID, its what they start at.

At Uptie Level 2, you gain access to your ID's passives.

At Uptie Level 3, you gain access to your ID's Skill 3's.

At Uptie Level 4, you get an all around improvement, maxing out your identity in potential.

You are also able to uptie E.G.O's, improving their clashing potential and effects.

## Combat Basics
Combat is a little complicated, but I'll try to make it easy to understand.

In combat there will be two sides, your side, with the sinner's on the left, and the enemies side, who is typically on the right, and what you'll see on the bottom side of the screen is this


![[Pasted image 20261006153454.png]]

This looks complicated, but its simple.

Each sinner has 2 skills available to choose from, lets use sinclair as an example on this.

![[Pasted image 20261006153515.png]]

The bar surrounding his portrait is a physical representation of his health, with the number being what his health is currently at.

He also has small lines, which dictate stagger points, if his health ever went under that bar due to a skill, he would stagger and become unable to use his skills next turn.

He also has a little blue number with a circle around it, which is sanity (or SP)

SP is very important, as it dictates your chance for rolling heads/tails. Initially you start with a 50/50 each coin at 0sp, but each point increases or decreases the chance to hit heads by 1%, so at 45SP (the cap) you would have a 95% chance to roll heads, and at -45SP you have a 95% chance to roll tails, so theres always a bit of RNG involved

![[Pasted image 20261006154039.png]]

In some scenarios, you may just have to drag the chain along the skills, which are automatically targetted and out of control for unfocused encounters.

In focused encounters, you would manually select the skill and drag it to the skill you want to clash again, which provides that feedback. Which is known as the forecast.

The forecast provides an idea for how it will turn out for your sinner, it comes in 5 categories:

Dominating, Favored, Neutral, Struggling, Hopeless.

Dominating means theres a high chance you'll succeed, favored means theres a decent chance you'll win, neutral is anyones game, struggling means theres a decent chance you'll lose and hopeless is practically guaranteed to lose.

You want to go for dominating clashes to ensure you're given the best chance.

Winning clashes is also how you raise your sanity, so it might be good to sacrifice big skills early on in favour for SP generation to make it easier with your weaker skills.

 Once you're happy, just press the start gear at the end and the combat will begin, that is all out of your control so just watch and see what happens

There are also 2 buttons on the right, win rate and damage, you should NEVER use these as they dont account for some conditions that may actually prevent you from winning the clash with the skill you use

You should only ever use them for luxcavations or short-filler fights.
